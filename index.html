Research

I'm a fourth-year math undergrad at POSTECH and an undergraduate researcher in the Computer Vision Lab, advised by Prof. Minsu Cho. I work on geometric representation learning for 3D vision.

Shapes show up in arbitrary poses, half observed, and often symmetric enough that there isn't one right answer. Most models learn to cope with this from augmented data, which works until it doesn't. I'd rather put the rotation structure into the architecture so it holds by construction, and spend the model's capacity on the actual problem.

Lately that means spectral methods on SO(3) and on the sphere. I like them because you can see what's happening: an object's symmetry group tells you which components it can represent at all, so failures are something you read off instead of guess at. Orientation estimation is where I started. Registration, shape correspondence, and fracture assembly are what I'm looking at now.

I came to vision from pure math, and that's still how I read a problem.

Publications

Distributional Estimation of 3D Object Orientation

Minjoon Kim, Chunghyun Park, Minsu Cho

NeurIPS 2026 Sydney, December 2026

The canonical orientation of a 3D object usually isn't unique. Many objects admit several equally valid frames, and under rotational symmetry those frames form an orbit of the symmetry group, which no single-output regressor can represent. We predict a continuous multi-modal distribution over SO(3) instead, through a truncated Wigner-D expansion that is equivariant by construction and assumes nothing about which symmetry group an object has. It outperforms recent equivariant regressors and fixed-quotient classifiers on the ShapeNet orientation benchmark, most clearly on categories with continuous rotational symmetry.
